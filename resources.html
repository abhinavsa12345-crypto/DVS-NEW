References

Each source is mapped to the specific element of the model it supports. DOI links are permanent identifiers.

Decision theory

  • Keeney, R. L. & Raiffa, H. (1976). Decisions with Multiple Objectives: Preferences and Value Tradeoffs. Wiley. [overview] — the additive value function and its independence condition.
  • Edwards, W. (1977). “How to use multiattribute utility measurement for social decision making.” IEEE Trans. SMC, 7(5), 326–340. [doi] — SMART; the anchored-scale method used here.
  • Pöyhönen, M. & Hämäläinen, R. P. (2001). “On the convergence of multiattribute weighting methods.” EJOR, 129(3), 569–585. [doi] — why the weights are treated as a prior.
  • Saaty, T. L. (1980). The Analytic Hierarchy Process. McGraw-Hill. [overview] — recommended upgrade path for weight derivation.

Sales-force sizing and call planning

  • Lodish, L. M. (1971). “CALLPLAN: An interactive salesman's call planning system.” Management Science, 18(4-II), 25–40. [doi] — origin of concave response and optimised call allocation.
  • Manchanda, P., Rossi, P. E. & Chintagunta, P. K. (2004). “Response Modeling with Nonrandom Marketing-Mix Variables.” JMR, 41(4), 467–478. [doi] — confirms concavity; documents that firms over-detail high-volume, low-response physicians.
  • Zoltners, A. A. & Sinha, P. (2005). “Sales Territory Design: Thirty Years of Modeling and Implementation.” Marketing Science, 24(3), 313–331. [doi] — territory design as bi-criteria optimisation.

Diffusion and adoption

  • Bass, F. M. (1969). “A New Product Growth for Model Consumer Durables.” Management Science, 15(5), 215–227. [doi] — the S-curve used to test the adoption ramp.
  • Rogers, E. M. (2003). Diffusion of Innovations, 5th ed. Free Press. [overview] — adopter categories underpinning Pillar 2.
  • Sultan, F., Farley, J. U. & Lehmann, D. R. (1990). “A Meta-Analysis of Applications of Diffusion Models.” JMR, 27(1), 70–77. [doi] — benchmark parameters p ≈ 0.03, q ≈ 0.38.

Opinion leadership

  • Coleman, J. S., Katz, E. & Menzel, H. (1966). Medical Innovation: A Diffusion Study. Bobbs-Merrill. — the Columbia tetracycline study; basis for Pillar 3.
  • Van den Bulte, C. & Lilien, G. L. (2001). “Medical Innovation Revisited: Social Contagion versus Marketing Effort.” AJS, 106(5), 1409–1435. [doi] — the essential corrective on contagion effects.
  • Nair, H. S., Manchanda, P. & Bhatia, T. (2010). “Asymmetric Social Interactions in Physician Prescription Behavior.” JMR, 47(5), 883–895. [doi] — a credible, bounded estimate of peer influence.

Scheduling and concentration

  • Graham, R. L. (1969). “Bounds on Multiprocessing Timing Anomalies.” SIAM J. Appl. Math., 17(2), 416–429. [doi] — the LPT worst-case guarantee.
  • Gini, C. (1912) / Lorenz, M. O. (1905). Concentration measurement. [overview] — the test of whether targeting is warranted.
  • Pareto, V. (1896). Cours d'Économie Politique. [overview] — the concentration principle operationalised here.

Regulatory

  • Department of Pharmaceuticals, Government of India (2024). Uniform Code for Pharmaceutical Marketing Practices (UCPMP) 2024, notified 12 March 2024. [published summary] — the binding constraint on all promotional spend.
  • Apex Laboratories v. DCIT, Supreme Court of India (2022); Explanation 3 to s.37(1), Income-tax Act 1961 — non-compliant spend disallowed as business expenditure.
