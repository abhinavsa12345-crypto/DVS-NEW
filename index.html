Overview

What this project does

A pharmaceutical company launching a new molecule has far more prescribers than it can possibly engage. This project replaces guesswork with a predictive scoring model that decides which doctors to invest in, how much to invest in each, and proves the resulting plan is physically deliverable.

1 Predict

Every doctor is scored on five weighted dimensions to produce a composite Doctor Value Score — a single, rankable index of expected commercial value.

2 Prioritize

Scores band doctors into four tiers. Roughly 200 of 600 prescribers are identified as driving the majority of prescription volume.

3 Optimize

Visit frequency and budget are allocated by marginal return, concentrating 4.15× more spend on the doctors who will actually prescribe.

What makes this different. Every formula is derived from established theory rather than asserted — Multi-Attribute Utility Theory for the scoring function, sales-response optimisation for the visit ladder, makespan scheduling for territory design. The model also tests its own premise and reports where it is weakest.
The Core Formula

The Doctor Value Score

Five dimensions, weighted by how directly each determines realised prescriptions, combined into one index on a 0–10 scale.

DVS = (P₁×0.30) + (P₂×0.25) + (P₃×0.20) + (P₄×0.15) + (P₅×0.10)
Σ wᵢ = 1 · anchored 0–10 rubrics · attainable range [1.75, 10.00]

Headline Findings

Results at a glance

Feasibility proved, not assumed

The derived plan needs 750 calls per month against 1,188 available — 63% utilisation, with headroom for chemist, hospital and administrative work. It still works at 76% under a pessimistic capacity assumption.

Investment concentrated where it pays

The same ₹30 lakh budget spent 4.15× harder on the top tier — ₹20,770 per Platinum doctor against ₹5,000 under uniform allocation, funded by withdrawing effort from doctors who will not prescribe.

Tiered frequency is an optimisation result

Because detailing response is concave and capacity binding, the optimum equalises marginal return per call. A tier ladder discretely approximates that optimum — turning a management preference into a derived conclusion.

The model tests its own premise

A Gini coefficient of 0.21 confirms value is concentrated enough to justify differentiated targeting. Had value been evenly spread, the approach would be unjustified — and the model would say so.
