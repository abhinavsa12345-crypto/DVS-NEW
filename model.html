Section 02

The Predictive Model

A five-pillar composite index that converts doctor attributes into a single, rankable measure of expected commercial value.

A composite predictive index

Every doctor is evaluated on five dimensions simultaneously and combined into a single Doctor Value Score. The weights reflect how directly each dimension determines realised prescriptions, discounted by how reliably it can be measured.

DVS = Σ wᵢ · sᵢ
= (P₁×0.30)+(P₂×0.25)+(P₃×0.20)+(P₄×0.15)+(P₅×0.10)
subject to Σ wᵢ = 1, wᵢ > 0, sᵢ ∈ [0,10]

The five pillars

P₁

Prescription Potential

Relevant patients per month — scored on the target condition, not total footfall. A dermatologist seeing 100 patients daily scores zero for a cardiology molecule.

30%weight
P₂

Adoption Propensity

Willingness to try a new molecule within the launch window. Grounded in Rogers' adopter categories: early adopter, early majority, late majority, laggard.

25%weight
P₃

Influence & Network Power

Whether the doctor shapes peers' prescribing. Operationally defined by peer nomination and referral counts rather than job title.

20%weight
P₄

Accessibility

Whether the doctor can realistically be met. A low score triggers a change of channel — senior manager, peer-to-peer, digital — rather than disqualification.

15%weight
P₅

Competitive Vulnerability

Whether a competitor's hold can be broken. Lowest weight because it is the most volatile and least reliably observed variable.

10%weight

Scoring rubrics

Each pillar uses anchored descriptors so that two representatives scoring the same doctor arrive at the same number. This is what makes the index reproducible.

Worked example

Dr. Rajesh Kumar, Cardiologist — 55 relevant cardiac patients per month, a known early adopter who has tried three new brands in two years, a local conference speaker, available before OPD hours, currently using a competitor brand but open to alternatives.

DVS = 7.85 falls in the band [6.0, 8.0) and classifies as GOLD — 2 visits per month, ₹6,222 per quarter. The doctor sits 0.15 points below the Platinum threshold and is flagged for review at the next recalibration.

Segmentation into four tiers

Scores band into four operational tiers, each mapping to a distinct engagement mode: intensive personal, regular personal, periodic personal, and digital-only.

Half-open intervals. Bands are implemented as [lo, hi) so that every possible score falls into exactly one tier. Written as “6.0–7.9” and “8.0–10.0”, a score of 7.95 would belong to no tier — and because the weight vector against integer scores generates values on a 0.05 lattice, such scores are genuinely attainable.
